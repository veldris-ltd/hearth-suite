# The Hearth Suite Ethos

### What we believe, and why we bothered

*A Veldris Ltd. open-source project*

---

My grandmother kept the family accounts in Microsoft Works. Birthdays, the church rota, a running total of what my grandfather spent at the bookmaker's that she pretended not to track. The disc came free with the computer. She never made an account, never paid a penny after the first, and it never once occurred to her that the letters she typed were anyone's business but hers. Why would it? The program sat on her desk. The words went onto her disk. That was the whole arrangement.

I've been thinking about that arrangement a lot, because the industry quietly dismantled it while we were all looking at our phones.

Somewhere in the last fifteen years the deal changed and nobody really voted on it. The thing you bought became the thing you rent. The program on your desk became a tenant in someone else's building. And your documents, your divorce letter, your tax return, your terrible novel, stopped being your documents and became *data*: a thing to be scanned and scored and fed to a model so that a company you've never met can sell a slightly better guess about you to a company you'll never meet. The cursor blinks in the same place it always did. The room it sits in belongs to strangers now.

Hearth Suite is us trying to give the old arrangement back.

That's the entire idea, and I don't want to dress it up. We took the thing that made Works quietly wonderful, that an ordinary person could sit down and just *get on with it*, and we rebuilt it for a world that has earned a lot less trust. Then we added the two things the old machine couldn't manage: it keeps real secrets, and it can actually help you think. Both of those happen on your own computer. Neither of them phones home.

We call it Hearth Suite because the hearth is the part of a house where the fire is, and the fire is yours.

---

## The things we won't trade away

What follows isn't a feature list. It's the short list of promises that, if we break them, mean we've stopped being Hearth Suite and become just another company that started well. Print them out. Check them against what we ship.

**Your work is yours. Full stop, no asterisk.**
Not ours. Not "ours to process on your behalf, as described in section 9 of a document you didn't read." Yours. The files sit on your machine. The keys that lock them are held by you and nobody else. We never see your words, so we can't sell them, can't train on them, can't hand them to anyone with a warrant or a chequebook. When you ask "who can read this?", we built Hearth Suite so the true answer is always: the people you picked, and no one else. Everything below is just consequences of this.

**It has to disappear.**
Good tools get out of the way. My grandmother needed no manual and attended no onboarding webinar. She opened the program and was already working, and that was the genius of it. So: encrypting a document is one tap. Asking for a formula is one sentence in plain English. The clever, powerful, complicated machinery is all in there for the people who want it, but it sits underneath, quietly, and it never makes you prove you understand it before it lets you start. If a first-time user can't be useful inside a minute, we've failed, and the fix is our problem, not theirs.

**Buy the thing, own the thing.**
We don't think you should pay rent every month for software that's just sitting on a computer you already own. Buy Hearth Suite Pro and it's yours, the way Works was yours. We only ask for money on a recurring basis when something genuinely costs us money on a recurring basis, running a server, settling a marketplace sale, and never for the simple act of opening the program. The free core is free forever and it's a real, complete tool, not a demo with the good bits removed.

**Privacy isn't the expensive trim. It's the floor.**
A lot of companies sell your safety back to you as the premium tier. We find that a bit grubby. The free version of Hearth Suite, the one that costs nothing, is end-to-end encrypted and runs its intelligence locally, because keeping you safe isn't a feature we'd dream of charging extra for. We went with post-quantum encryption for an unglamorous reason: "private now" should still mean "private when your kids are grown," including against the kind of computer that hasn't been built yet but probably will be.

**You shouldn't have to hand over your privacy to get a smart assistant.**
Here's the trick every other suite plays. They offer you a clever helper, but the helper lives in their data centre and eats your files to do its job. You can be private or you can be assisted, pick one. We weren't willing to make you choose. Ember, that's our assistant, runs on your machine. It writes, summarises, untangles, answers, and not one word you give it ever leaves. We built it so that the day some future version of us gets greedy and wants to sell your documents to an AI, they'll find we made it physically impossible years ago.

**Open, because "trust us" is not good enough.**
Hearth Suite's source is open. You don't have to take our word for where the locks are. You can go and read them, and so can people far more suspicious and far more qualified than you. If we ever drift, someone can pick the work up and carry it somewhere better. Open formats mean your work is never held hostage. Open code means our promises can be checked instead of believed. We'd rather be inspected than trusted, because a promise you can't verify is just an advertisement, and we're tired of those too.

**We're building this for the kitchen table.**
The kid finishing coursework at 1am. The plasterer doing an invoice with the telly on. The pensioner keeping everyone's addresses straight. The two-person charity printing a flyer it can't afford to outsource. That's who Works was for, and that's who we answer to first. Hearth Suite will get powerful, it already is, but the moment it gets too clever to serve the person at the kitchen table who just needs to finish one honest thing, we'll have lost the plot entirely.

**Quiet is the point.**
There is already enough software that pings, nags, upsells, and begs you not to leave. We're trying to make the opposite of that. No dark patterns. No fake urgency. No little dopamine hooks engineered to keep you "engaged." Hearth Suite does the job, steps back, and lets you close the laptop without a guilt-trip. We are not trying to maximise your time inside our app. We're trying to minimise it. If you finished fast and got on with your life, that's the win.

## Where the talk meets the build

An ethos that doesn't change what you ship is just a poster in the break room. So, concretely:

- You can install Hearth Suite and start working before you've told us your name. Most of the time, it's frankly none of our business.
- Sealing a document says "Only you and Maria can open this," not "AES-256-GCM envelope established." No keys to wrangle.
- Ember runs locally, by design, specifically so that selling your work later is a door we already bricked up.
- Hearth Suite Pro is bought once. Subscription is there if you'd rather, and only for the cloud bits, never for the software on your own disk.
- Your ancient `.wps` and `.wks` files still open. We're not going to abandon the documents the old program promised to keep.
- It's built to run on the computer you already have, not the one we wish you'd buy.
- "Free" isn't the cheese in a trap. The open core stands on its own. We'd genuinely rather have a big crowd we treated decently than a small one we wrung out.

---

## So, the promise

We're not out to win anything. We're not trying to topple the giants or own a category or get written up as disruptors. We're trying to hand a few honest tools back to the people who got quietly sold out when somebody decided their data was worth more than their trust.

If you sit down at Hearth Suite and, a minute later, you're just doing the thing you came to do, no account, no subscription you resent, no nagging worry that something somewhere is reading along, then we've kept faith with that free disc on my grandmother's desk, and with you.

Your work. Your fire. Yours alone.
